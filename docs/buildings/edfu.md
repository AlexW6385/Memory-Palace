# Temple of Horus at Edfu — dossier

Building id: `edfu` · Style: `egyptian` · District: Nile (Egypt)
Status of this dossier: L1, with the roof stairs of L2 (plan, section, structure, proportions, materials, light, music). Sections marked *L2/L3* are recorded now so later work does not need new research, but are not yet built.

Tags: **[stated]** = given by a cited source; **[inferred]** = our reasoning from the sources, the building type, or comparison with sister temples (Dendera, Kom Ombo, Philae), with the reasoning written next to it.

## 1. The real example

- **What:** the temple of Horus of Behdet (Horus Behdety), Edfu, Upper Egypt, on the west bank of the Nile between Luxor and Aswan. Ancient name *Behdet*; Greek *Apollinopolis Magna*. [stated: Wikipedia]
- **Builders and dates:** foundation 23 August 237 BC under Ptolemy III Euergetes; the naos (inner temple) finished and decorated first, the pronaos added after, the enclosure wall begun about 140 BC, the pylon decorated under Ptolemy IX–XII, and the whole completed under Ptolemy XII Auletes in 57 BC; the great cedar doors were hung in the last stage. [stated: Wikipedia; Madain Project]
- **Earlier phases:** an older temple stood on the site on an east–west axis; a ruined New Kingdom pylon (Ramesses I–II) lies just east of the present temple. The granite naos of Nectanebo II (30th Dynasty, 360–343 BC) was kept from the older temple and stands in the new sanctuary. [stated: Wikipedia; UNESCO tentative list]
- **Preservation:** the best preserved large temple in Egypt; roofs, walls, pylon and enclosure survive almost complete because the temple was buried in sand and silt and built over by the town until Mariette cleared it in the 1860s. [stated: general; Madain Project]

## 2. Type and typology

The canonical Ptolemaic cult temple ("house of the god"). From the river: pylon → open court with colonnades → pronaos (columned façade hall with screen walls) → inner hypostyle ("hall of appearances") with service rooms → offering hall with stairs to the roof → hall of the Ennead (vestibule) → barque sanctuary, a "house within the house", surrounded by a corridor and chapels → the whole naos and court enclosed by a high wall with an ambulatory between. The axis is a single straight line; **each hall is higher in floor, lower in ceiling and darker than the one before it**, so the god lives on the primeval mound in darkness. [stated: Arnold 1999 (type); egypt-uncovered; tripsinegypt — general principle in every source]

Sister temples on the same plan: Dendera (Hathor), Kom Ombo (double), Esna (pronaos only), Philae (Isis). Where Edfu sources are silent, these and the type decide. [inferred]

## 3. Orientation and site

- Axis **north–south**, entrance (pylon) facing **south**, sanctuary at the north. [stated: Wikipedia]
- In our world the plan keeps true orientation: local +z = south, the pylon on the south.
- The Nile is to the east of the town; the temple stands on the town mound. [stated: location] The approach in antiquity came from the south/south-east, where the birth house (mammisi) stands in front of the pylon, south-west. [stated: ancient-egypt.co.uk; Madain Project]

## 5. Section and structure

- Post-and-lintel throughout: columns carry architraves; roof slabs span from architrave to architrave (≈ 3–6 m). No arches or vaults. [stated: type]
- Heights (top of roof slab) [inferred, stepping down inward]: court colonnade 12.0, pronaos 15.4 (façade with cornice ~17.4), hypostyle 12.6, offering hall 11.4, Ennead 10.6, sanctuary 9.8, chapels and corridor 8.4. Enclosure wall 10.5 m with torus and cornice.
- Pylon: two battered towers (batter ≈ 1:12 per face, i.e. 3 m inset at 36 m), torus roll on the corners and under the cavetto cornice; a lower gate block between them crowned by its own cornice. Inside the towers: stairs and chambers lit by slits in the façade [stated: ancient-egypt.co.uk]; *L2*.
- Four vertical niches in the façade (two per tower) for cedar flagpoles, held by clamps passing through windows above. [stated: Borghino; geomerid] Niche depth ~0.7 m, width 1.6 m, rising to 2/3 of the tower height [inferred]. Masts about 40 m [inferred from tower height].

## 6. Sequence of spaces and use

1. **Forecourt and pylon.** Public space. Two granite falcons of Horus flank the gate [stated]. The pylon is the horizon (*akhet*): two mountains between which the sun rises [stated: type]; here the sun indeed comes up on the left of the gate in the morning.
2. **Court of offerings (peristyle).** Last place open to the sky; reached by people of standing at feasts; offerings laid out [stated: geomerid "public gathered"]. Columns paired across the axis, capitals all different down each side [stated: Baudelet "every column different"; egypt-uncovered "paired"]. Gates from the north corners lead into the ambulatory [stated].
3. **Pronaos.** Façade of columns with screen walls, so light falls only through the upper intercolumniations. Two small rooms built against the inside of the façade: a **library** ("House of Books", east) and a **robing room / chamber of consecrations** (west) [stated: egypt-uncovered]. The black granite falcon with the double crown stands at the pronaos entrance (one survives of a pair) [stated: Madain; Borghino]. Ceiling painted with astronomical scenes [stated: geomerid].
4. **Hall of appearances (inner hypostyle).** Twelve columns. Side rooms: on the west, the **laboratory** (recipes for unguents and incense are carved on its walls) and a chamber for solid offerings / treasury; on the east, a chamber for liquid offerings and the passage to the **well ("Nile room")** [stated: ancient-egypt.co.uk; egypt-uncovered]. Roof slits admit thin light [stated: Baudelet].
5. **Offering hall.** Daily offerings set out. Two stairs to the roof: one straight, one winding round a square core; at New Year the statue was carried up one and down the other to be united with the sun on the roof [stated: ancient-egypt.co.uk; egypt-uncovered]. **Built (L2) [inferred route]:** our sources do not give the stairs' exact courses, so each is modelled within its stair room (2.8 × 7.0 m, entered from the offering hall at its south end) as a dog-leg of four flights (risers ≈ 0.175 m) in two lanes either side of a spine wall, arriving on the offering-hall roof at 11.4 m; the stairwell is open to the sky over its upper flights. The roof is walkable at its stepped levels (chapels 9.2, Ennead 10.6, offering hall 11.4, hall of appearances 12.4 m) joined by short flights. To revise when a published survey of the stairs is studied. *Still L2: the New Year court and kiosk.*
6. **Hall of the Ennead (vestibule).** Doors to the chapel of **Min** (west) and the **linen room** (east) [stated: ancient-egypt.co.uk].
7. **Sanctuary.** Contains the granite naos of Nectanebo II [stated] with its gilded wooden statue of Horus (~60 cm) [stated: egypt-uncovered], and before it the granite pedestal on which the portable barque rested [stated: Wikipedia "barque sanctuary"; Madain "replica barque"]. Lit only by three small apertures in the roof [stated: Madain]. Only the high priest entered.
8. **Corridor of mysteries and chapels.** Around the sanctuary; chapels of Khonsu, Hathor, the Throne of Re, the Spread Wings (Mehit), Osiris (rear), the Tomb of Osiris, the West, the Throne of the Gods, and Horus the Victor (the chapel now holding the replica barque) [stated: names from egypt-uncovered and ancient-egypt.co.uk; their exact positions are not given by our sources: arrangement **[inferred]** — West side front→back: Khonsu, Hathor, Throne of Re; rear west→east: Osiris, Tomb of Osiris, the West; east side back→front: Spread Wings, Throne of the Gods, Victor]. 
9. **Ambulatory.** Between naos and enclosure, open to the sky; the inner face of the west enclosure wall carries the Sacred Drama of Horus and Seth [stated: Wikipedia]. Steps beneath the outer wall on the east lead to the **nilometer** [stated: ancient-egypt.co.uk]. *L2.*
10. **Mammisi** (birth house), south-west in front of the pylon, perpendicular to the axis, with an ambulatory of columns with Bes abaci [stated: Madain]. *Separate building, Phase 2/3.*

## 7. Materials and finishes

- **Sandstone** (Nubian sandstone from Gebel el-Silsila [inferred — the standard source for Upper Egyptian Ptolemaic temples]), buff to golden; ashlar of courses about 0.5 m, blocks 1–1.5 m long [inferred]; floors of large flags. Columns built of drums. [material stated: Wikipedia]
- **Granite:** falcons (black/grey), the naos (grey), the barque pedestal (black). [stated]
- **Cedar of Lebanon:** great gate doors (hung 56 BC) [stated: Madain], flagpoles [inferred], shrine doors.
- Bronze fittings on doors; gilding on the barque and the god's image [stated: type].
- Original paint: reliefs were painted (traces remain in sheltered places); ceilings blue with yellow stars and astronomical figures [stated: geomerid; type]. Present-day state is weathered stone, ceilings of the hypostyle blackened by later fires [stated: Madain]. **Decision:** we show the temple as **completed and in use (57 BC)**, but with stone as stone — paint at L3 only where sources describe it, since its full extent is uncertain [inferred].

## 8. Decorative programme

*L2/L3.* Recorded for later:
- Pylon: Ptolemy XII smiting enemies before Horus and Hathor, colossal, one on each tower [stated: Madain; geomerid]; winged sun disc on the gate lintel [type].
- Every wall: registers of offering scenes, king before the gods, with columns of hieroglyphic text; bands of dedication text at the base and top [stated: type; Madain "four rows of reliefs"].
- Hypostyle: astronomical band, frieze of the king's names guarded by falcons [stated: Madain].
- Ceilings: vultures with spread wings along the axis, stars elsewhere, astronomical scenes in the pronaos [stated/type].
- Ambulatory west wall: the Sacred Drama, Horus spearing Seth as a hippopotamus [stated: Wikipedia; Baudelet].
- Staircase walls: the New Year procession of priests carrying the god [stated: egypt-uncovered].
- Building texts on the enclosure's exterior describe the founding and the temple as the Island of Creation [stated: Wikipedia].
- Lion-headed rain spouts high on the outer walls [type, Dendera; inferred for Edfu].

## 9. Lighting

- Court open to the sky.
- Pronaos lit through the open upper intercolumniations above the screen walls and the door.
- Hypostyle: narrow slits in the roof [stated] and the door.
- Offering hall: roof light shafts [inferred, as at Dendera].
- Sanctuary: three small apertures in the roof [stated].
- Chapels: dark; lamps (oil lamps on stands) [inferred]. Lamps are placed in the corridor and chapels at L1 so the inner rooms can be seen.

## 10. Surroundings

The Nile region of the world (docs/world.md) is the temple's setting.

- **The town of Behdet** [stated: the town mound lies "just a few meters west" of the temple, 365 m N–S × 168 m E–W, now 10–15 m above the temple threshold; parts of the town lie south and east of the temple — Yale/ISAC Tell Edfu Project, https://egyptology.yale.edu/node/379]. In 57 BC the mound was lower [inferred]; we make it 5.5 m. Houses of the Graeco-Roman types [stated: tower houses of two or more storeys reached by a central stair, and houses with courtyards — UCL Digital Egypt, Karanis; Egyptian Streets, "Living as an Egyptian during Greco-Roman times"]: mud brick, flat roofs of palm trunks behind parapets, small windows high up, doors with timber lintels [inferred: type]. Laid out in blocks between straight streets [inferred from the planned towns of the period].
- **Flood plain and fields** between the town and the river; date and doum palms, sycamore figs, acacias and tamarisks; papyrus and reeds at the water's edge [inferred: the vegetation of Upper Egypt in antiquity and today].
- **The processional way** from the river to the pylon, on which the god's barque came and went at the feasts (the Feast of the Beautiful Meeting, when Hathor of Dendera arrived by river) [stated: type; feast — general].
- **The desert** west of the valley behind its escarpment; dunes banked against it [inferred: setting of Edfu between the Nile and the Western Desert].
- *Later (L4):* the quay and its stair, the mammisi south-west of the pylon, dovecotes and granaries, people and animals.

## 11. Music

Sources on Ptolemaic temple music: temple musicians (singers, harpists, sistrum players — the sistrum sacred to Hathor, Horus's consort), the cult hymns sung at the daily ritual (Morning Hymn to awaken the god), feasts with drums, clappers and double pipes (aulos-like). The scale system is not recorded; reconstructions use pentatonic and heptatonic modes; harp tunings (angular harp, 10–20 strings) suggest diatonic or anhemitonic tunings. [stated: general musicology (Manniche, *Music and Musicians in Ancient Egypt*, 1991); scale **[inferred]**]

Our synthesis (src/buildings/edfu/music.js):
- Mode: anhemitonic pentatonic on D (D E G A C), with a drone on D and A.
- Outdoors / court: angular harp (plucked string, Karplus–Strong), slow free rhythm, frame drum and clappers on festive pulses, sistrum shimmer at phrase ends.
- Pronaos and hypostyle: harp sparser, low sung-like drone (filtered sawtooth with formant), longer reverb.
- Sanctuary and chapels: almost silent: a single low drone and occasional sistrum.
- Tempo ~56 bpm, free phrasing.
