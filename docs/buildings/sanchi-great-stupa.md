## 1. The real example

- **What:** the Great Stupa (Stupa No. 1) on the hill of Sanchi, near Vidisha in Madhya Pradesh: the oldest stone structure in India and the most complete early Buddhist monument, part of a World Heritage site (1989). [stated: Wikipedia; UNESCO]
- **Dates:** a brick stupa of Ashoka (3rd century BCE) over relics, about half the present diameter; doubled in size and cased in stone, with its terrace, stairway and railings, in the 2nd century BCE (ground railing c. 150 BCE, stairway balustrade 125–100 BCE); the four gateways added in the 1st century BCE and 1st century CE, the south one first (inscription of Satakarni's foreman Ananda), then the north, the east and the west. [stated: Wikipedia]
- **State shown:** as completed with its gateways, in the 1st century CE, before the Gupta additions (the seated Buddhas at the entrances, c. 450 CE, and Temple 17) [stated: dates — Wikipedia; inditales]; restored to whole where the 19th-century restoration (1881–1919) rebuilt what had fallen. [stated: Britannica]

## 2. Type and typology

A stupa: a hemispherical dome (*anda*) over relics, on a circular terrace (*medhi*) for walking round it, reached by a double stairway; on the dome's flattened top a square railing (*harmika*) round the shaft (*yashti*) of a triple parasol (*chattra*); round the whole a ground railing (*vedika*) with four gateways (*torana*) at the cardinal points. The railings and gateways turn wooden construction into stone: posts with lens-shaped crossbars and a rounded coping; gateways of two square posts with capitals carrying three architraves with volute ends. [stated: Britannica; UNESCO; Wikipedia]

## 3. Orientation and site

- On the top of a hill rising from the plain, about 10 km from Vidisha; about fifty monuments on the hill. [stated: Wikipedia; UNESCO] The gateways at the cardinal points; the stairway on the south, the main entrance. [stated: inditales; Wikipedia]
- In our world: in a new region, India, north of Heian Japan over a pass: the plain of Malwa with the flat-topped hill of Sanchi, the stupa on its top, the river beyond; dry deciduous forest on the slopes, fields below. [inferred]

## 5. Section and structure

A rubble and brick core cased in dressed sandstone, the drum faced and its top paved; the railings and gateways of sandstone in pieces joined like timber (mortise and tenon). [stated: Wikipedia; UNESCO]

## 6. Sequence of spaces and use

1. **The climb** up the hill to the south gateway, past the Ashoka pillar. [stated: positions]
2. **The ground walk** (*pradakshina*) between the railing and the drum, clockwise. [stated: Britannica]
3. **Up the stairway** to the terrace and round again under the dome. [stated]

## 7. Materials and finishes

Buff and pinkish sandstone; the dome probably plastered white and painted [inferred: lime plaster traces at many stupas; shown as pale lime plaster]; the parasols of stone. [stated: sandstone — UNESCO]

## 8. Decorative programme (L3)

The gateways' reliefs: the Buddha's life in symbols (tree, wheel, footprints, empty throne), the Jatakas, Ashoka's visit, the war of the relics; yakshis as brackets; elephants, lions and dwarfs as capitals; the donors' inscriptions on the railing.

## 9. Lighting

Open sky; the region's winter afternoon sun in the south-west throws the gateways' shadows across the walk. [inferred]
