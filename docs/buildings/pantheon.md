# The Pantheon, Rome — dossier

Building id: `pantheon` · Style: `roman` · District: Rome
Status of this dossier: L1–L2. Tags: **[stated]** by a cited source; **[inferred]** our reasoning.

## 1. The real example

- Temple of all the gods in the Campus Martius, Rome. Agrippa's first building (29–19 BC) burned in AD 80 and 110; the present building was begun c. AD 114 (Trajanic brick stamps) and finished and dedicated under Hadrian c. AD 125–128; the inscription of Agrippa was kept on the new façade. Designer unknown. Consecrated as a church in 609. [stated: Wikipedia; pantheon.museum]
- **State shown:** as completed under Hadrian, c. AD 128 — bronze roof tiles, bronze portico trusses, the original attic, porphyry in the aediculae.

## 2. Type

A Corinthian temple portico (octastyle, three aisles) joined by a rectangular intermediate block to a domed rotunda — the circle of the cosmos with the sky open at its crown. The interior is a sphere of 43.3 m set on a cylinder: the floor to the oculus equals the diameter, and the springing of the dome is at half the height. [stated: pantheon.museum; Wikipedia]

## 3. Orientation and site

Façade faces north [stated: Wikipedia]. In our world: on the plain of the Roman region (docs/world.md), the rotunda at (−20, −1080), its portico facing north onto its forecourt; the ancient forecourt was a long rectangular square closed by porticoes, and the portico was reached by seven steps (1.30 m) [stated: Wikipedia; archeoroma].

## 5. Structure

Concrete of lime and pozzolana with hand-laid aggregate that lightens upward (travertine, brick, tuff, scoria), brick-faced drum with relieving arches, eight piers between the niches, the dome thinning toward the open oculus; compression ring of bipedales around it. [stated]

## 6. Spaces and use

1. Forecourt (colonnaded square), seven steps up to the portico. [stated]
2. Portico: columns in three aisles; the central aisle leads to the bronze doors; statue niches at the ends of the side aisles. [stated]
3. Rotunda: statues of the gods in the niches and aediculae [inferred: type]; the disc of sunlight from the oculus moving across the coffers and walls through the day. [stated: pantheon.museum]

## 7. Materials and finishes

Granite shafts (grey, pink), Pentelic capitals and bases; interior marbles: pavonazzetto, giallo antico, porphyry, africano, greco scritto [stated]. Exterior brick originally faced with stucco above a marble-faced lower zone [inferred: common reconstructions]; dome roof gilded bronze tiles [stated: "gilded bronze tiles were stripped in 663", pantheon.museum].

## 8. Decorative programme (L3)

Coffer rosettes (conjectural gilded bronze) [stated: conjecture]; pediment relief (eagle in wreath, gilded bronze) [stated: probable]; the attic's original porphyry panels and pilasters [stated].

## 9. Light

Only the oculus and the open door: a 9 m disc of sun moving across the dome and walls; the rest lit by its reflection. [stated]

## 10. Surroundings

Forecourt with porticoes on three sides [stated: archeoroma; Wikipedia]. **Built (L2):** a travertine square 80 m wide reaching about 100 m north of the portico, closed by porticoes of granite Corinthian columns 7.6 m high in front of a back wall, with lean-to roofs, and a gateway in the north portico on the axis [inferred: proportions and order of the porticoes]. Around: the plain of the Campus Martius with stone pines, cypresses and holm oaks; hills to the south and north (docs/world.md). *L4: the streets and buildings of the Campus Martius.*

**In the city (2026-10-28):** the forecourt opens north onto the north street of the compact city; the Roman district round it has blocks of tiled houses, stone pines, cypresses and oleanders in its gardens.

## 11. Music

Roman temple music: the tibia (double reed pipe) played at sacrifice, the cornu and tuba in processions, the lyre; the hydraulis (water organ) in public spectacle. Greek theory (modes) in use. [stated: general musicology; e.g. Wille, *Musica Romana*] Our synthesis: Lydian species on F, tibia melody and drone in the portico and forecourt, the lyre in the rotunda with a very long reverberation (the real rotunda rings for many seconds).
