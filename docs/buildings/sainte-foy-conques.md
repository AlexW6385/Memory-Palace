## 1. The real example

- **What:** the abbey church of the Benedictine monastery of Conques in the Rouergue (Aveyron), which kept the relics of the child martyr Sainte Foy (Saint Faith) of Agen — brought there by theft in 866 — and became one of the great stops on the pilgrimage road to Santiago de Compostela (the *via Podiensis* from Le Puy). [stated: wikipedia; fr-academic]
- **Dates:** begun between 1041 and 1052 under abbot Odolric on the site of a Carolingian church; the chevet probably finished before his death in 1065; the sculpted parts of the transept about 1070–1080; the tribunes at the end of the 11th century; the nave and the west portal completed at the beginning of the 12th century (Structurae gives 1045–1120). The cloister was built by abbot Bégon III at the turn of the 11th and 12th centuries. [stated: fr-academic; POP Mérimée IA12000102; structurae]
- **Later:** the lantern dome over the crossing rebuilt at the end of the 15th century; the abbey burnt by Protestants in 1568; the cloister largely demolished about 1830; restored after Prosper Mérimée's visit (listed 1840); the two west towers rebuilt in the 19th century; windows by Pierre Soulages (1987–1994). UNESCO 1998 (Routes of Santiago). [stated: wikipedia; POP; fr-academic]
- **State shown:** as completed about 1120, with the cloister of Bégon complete and the church's sculpture painted. Not shown: the 15th-century lantern (a Romanesque octagonal lantern of the same size is shown [inferred]), the 19th-century towers (two square towers of the same place and size are shown, with low stone pyramids [inferred]), the Soulages glass (plain, slightly tinted glass in its place [inferred: 12th-century windows were small and lightly glazed]).

## 2. Type and typology

A **pilgrimage church** of the type shared with Saint-Martin of Tours, Saint-Martial of Limoges, Saint-Sernin of Toulouse and Santiago de Compostela [stated: POP groups it with Saint-Sernin and Santiago]: a Latin cross; a nave with aisles; a large **aisled transept**; **tribunes** (galleries) over the aisles of nave and transept; a **chevet with an ambulatory and radiating chapels**, so that crowds of pilgrims could walk round the choir and the relics without disturbing the monks' office; a continuous **barrel vault** over the nave, buttressed by the **half-barrels** of the tribunes; no clerestory in the nave. Conques is the oldest and the most compact of the group. [stated: wikipedia; structurae; fr-academic]

## 3. Orientation and site

- Apse to the east, entrance by the west portal under the Last Judgment. [stated: wikipedia]
- On a terrace cut into a steep slope above a gorge; the transept is longer than the nave because the slope left no room westward. [stated: fr-academic] The village climbs the slope above and below the abbey.
- In our world: a new district, Rouergue, at the west edge of the city beyond Francia; the slope rises to the north behind the church; the cloister lies south of the nave on the terrace; the west parvis faces a lane along the city's edge (docs/world.md). [inferred]

## 5. Section and structure

Ashlar on rubble cores: walls, piers and vaults all of stone [stated: structurae]. The nave's tunnel vault (about 0.60 m thick) is carried on transverse arches over the piers; its thrust is taken by the quadrant (half-barrel) vaults of the tribunes along nave and transept, which carry it down to the outer walls; the aisles are barrel vaulted (originally stuccoed) [stated: wikipedia; structurae]. The crossing is an octagon set in a square on squinches, with radiating ribs and angels in the squinches [stated: wikipedia]. Roofs of schist slabs (*lauzes*; POP: slate) on the vaults' extrados and on timber [stated: POP; inferred: lauze tradition of the Rouergue].

## 6. Sequence of spaces and use

1. **The lanes of the village** and the small **parvis** before the west front; the pilgrims arrived from the road from Le Puy. [stated: pilgrimage; parvis inferred]
2. **The west portal:** under the **tympanum of the Last Judgment** — Christ in majesty in the centre, the elect on his right, the damned on his left, Michael weighing souls, angels blowing horns in the top corners; Sainte Foy kneeling before the hand of God. A deep round-arched archivolt frames it. [stated: wikipedia; fr-academic]
3. **The nave:** high, narrow and dim between the piers, the light coming from the aisles and tribunes. [stated: structure; inferred: light]
4. **The crossing,** suddenly brighter under the lantern. **The transept,** whose aisles let the crowds pass round. [stated: transept aisles for pilgrim traffic — fr-academic]
5. **The choir** with the relics behind its iron grilles (12th century; by tradition forged from the fetters of prisoners freed by the saint), the monks' office; **the ambulatory** round it, from which the pilgrims saw the reliquary statue of Sainte Foy, and its **chapels** with their altars. [stated: wikipedia; fr-academic]
6. **The tribunes,** reached by stairs in the thickness of the walls (a spiral stair in masonry is recorded [stated: POP]), where pilgrims could keep vigil.
7. **The cloister** south of the nave, with the monks' buildings round it and the serpentine basin in its garden; the **refectory** (later the treasury). [stated: fr-academic]

## 7. Materials and finishes

- **Stone:** local **red sandstone** in the lower parts up to the level of the chapels, **yellow limestone** (from Lunel) above; **grey schist** rubble in the walls of other campaigns; dressed ashlar. [stated: POP Mérimée] Inside the same stones, bare, with the capitals and the tympanum painted [stated: traces of colour — wikipedia].
- **Roofs:** schist slabs (lauzes), grey; ridge and hips of stone. [stated: POP "ardoise"; inferred: lauzes]
- **Floor:** stone flags. [inferred]
- **Grilles:** wrought iron round the choir. [stated]
- **Windows:** small, round-headed, glazed with plain, slightly tinted glass in lead cames. [inferred]

## 9. Light

Orientation east–west. The nave without clerestory is dim; light falls from the aisles' and tribunes' windows across the piers; the crossing is lit from above by the lantern's eight windows; the choir by its clerestory and the ambulatory's and chapels' windows behind the columns of the hemicycle. Oil lamps hanging in the choir and before the chapels' altars; candles before the saint [inferred: Benedictine practice]. Baked lamps: two in the choir, one in each chapel and apsidiole, two in the nave.

## 10. Surroundings

The village of Conques on its slope: houses of schist rubble with roofs of lauzes, steep, with small windows, the upper storeys sometimes framed in timber and plastered (the timber-framed houses now standing are mostly of the 15th–16th centuries; in 1120 most houses were of stone, lower) [inferred: vernacular of the Rouergue]. Narrow lanes stepping down the slope; chestnut trees and walnuts, oaks on the slopes, vines and gardens on terraces; box and broom. [inferred] **Built (L2):** the parvis, the cloister with its galleries of twin arches and the basin, the refectory as a block, and the district's houses (`house_rouergue`, `src/world/city.js`) on the slope rising to the north. *L4: the abbey's other buildings, the Porte de Vinzelle, the bridge over the Dourdou.*

## 11. Music

The **pilgrims' songs** and the monastic office of the late 11th and early 12th century: Gregorian chant in the church modes, and the early polyphony of the south of France — the **Aquitanian versus** and organum of the Saint-Martial manuscripts, a chant voice held below a florid upper voice — and the pilgrim songs gathered in the Codex Calixtinus of Santiago (*Dum pater familias*, the "Ultreia" refrain). [stated: forms and repertories — general music history] Instruments: the **organistrum** (a hurdy-gurdy for two players, carved on Romanesque portals) giving a drone, the **vielle**, bells. Tempo slow and even. In the open: the drone of the organistrum and the pilgrims' refrain, the bells of the abbey. In the church: chant over a drone, a second voice in organum; long resonance. [inferred: zoning]
