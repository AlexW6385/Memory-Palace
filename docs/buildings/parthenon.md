# The Parthenon, Athens — dossier

Building id: `parthenon` · Style: `greek` · District: Greece (the Acropolis)
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (plan, section, order, materials, light, statue massing, music). L3 items (sculpture, paint, refinements) are recorded for later.

Tags: **[stated]** by a cited source; **[inferred]** our reasoning, written next to it.

## 1. The real example

- **What:** the temple of Athena Parthenos on the Acropolis of Athens. [stated: Wikipedia]
- **Builders and dates:** begun 447 BC, the building substantially complete by 432 BC, decoration to at least 431; architects Iktinos and Kallikrates, the sculptural programme under Pheidias. The cult statue was dedicated in 438 BC. [stated: Wikipedia]
- **State shown:** completed, 432 BC.

## 2. Type and typology

Octastyle peripteral Doric temple: 8 columns on the ends, 17 on the flanks (46 in all), a double row at front and back (the six-column porches of the pronaos and opisthodomos stand behind the peristyle), the cella divided into two rooms — the east naos with the statue and a Π-shaped two-tier interior colonnade, and the west room ("parthenon" proper, the treasury) with four Ionic columns. Doric and Ionic are mixed: an Ionic frieze runs around the top of the cella walls. [stated: Wikipedia; ancient-greece.org]

## 3. Orientation and site

- Long axis east–west, main entrance at the east. [stated: type; Wikipedia]
- On the south side of the Acropolis rock. In our world the Acropolis is a rock plateau 300 × 140 m, 40 m above the plain, with cliffs on three sides and the way up from the west (docs/world.md). [The real rock rises about 70 m; we keep 40 m so the climb stays walkable — decision in DESIGN.md §10.]

## 5. Section and structure

Post and lintel in marble: architraves (epistyle) of three parallel beams spanning column to column, the frieze and geison above, marble ceiling beams and coffers across the pteron, a timber roof over the cella (later replaced by a steeper one [stated: Wikipedia]) covered with marble tiles [stated: Wikipedia — "ceiling is marble, a Cycladic feature"; tiles marble: type]. Columns of drums, held with wooden empolia; blocks joined with iron clamps set in lead. [stated: Wikipedia]

**Refinements** [stated: Wikipedia; ancient-greece.org; arXiv 2510.16831]: the stylobate curves up ≈ 6–10 cm toward the middle; columns lean inward ≈ 0.4°; corner columns thicker and closer; entasis ≈ 1.8 cm off straight. *L3: we build the order straight at L1–L2 (except entasis, which is in the shafts); curvature and inclination need every element above to follow and are recorded for L3.*

## 6. Sequence of spaces and use

1. **Approach** from the west, up the slope, along the north side of the temple, to the east front. [stated: type; Panathenaic way]
2. **East front:** the peristyle, then the pronaos porch of six columns, the great east door flanked by two windows. [stated]
3. **Naos:** the statue on its pedestal, framed on three sides by the two-tier colonnade; in front of it a shallow pool of water — Pausanias says water, not oil, was used at Athens to keep the ivory from the dry air. [stated: ancient-greece.org; Pausanias via the same; the pool's form inferred]
4. **West room ("the parthenon"):** treasury of the Delian League, entered only from the west porch (opisthodomos); four Ionic columns carry its ceiling. [stated: Wikipedia]
5. **Pteron:** the walkway under the peristyle ceiling, along which the Ionic frieze of the Panathenaic procession could be seen. [stated: theartnewbie; type]

## 7. Materials and finishes

- Pentelic marble throughout the visible building; limestone foundations. [stated: Wikipedia]
- Statue: gold (removable plates, 44 talents ≈ 1,151 kg) and ivory on a wooden core. [stated: Dinsmoor]
- Doors: wood sheathed with bronze [inferred: type].
- Paint: the mouldings, triglyphs, sculpture and coffers were coloured [stated: Wikipedia]. Shown as marble at L2; paint at L3.

## 8. Decorative programme (L3)

- Metopes: east gigantomachy, west amazonomachy, north the sack of Troy, south centauromachy (92). [subjects: standard; counts stated]
- Ionic frieze: the Panathenaic procession (160 m). [stated]
- Pediments: east the birth of Athena, west the contest of Athena and Poseidon; ≈ 50 figures. [stated]
- Statue: shield exterior amazonomachy, interior gigantomachy, sandal edges centauromachy, pedestal the birth of Pandora. [standard]
- Acroteria at the corners and apex of the pediments. [type]

## 9. Lighting

- Exterior in full sun; the pteron in shade under the marble ceiling.
- Naos lit by the great east door (≈ 4.9 × 9.8 m) and the two windows (≈ 3 × 2.5 m) [stated: windows], the light falling on the gold and ivory across the pool, which reflected light upward onto the statue [inferred: commonly argued].
- West room: by its west door only.

## 10. Surroundings

The Acropolis plateau (docs/world.md): later the Propylaea at the west, the Erechtheion to the north, the altar of Athena east. *L4.*

## 11. Music

Classical Athens: the lyre and kithara (plucked strings) and the aulos (double reed pipe); hymns and processional music at the Panathenaia, where musical contests were held. Modes: Dorian (in the ancient sense, the octave species E–F–G–A–B–C–D–E with the semitone at the bottom), regarded as dignified and fitting for Athena. [stated: general musicology, e.g. M. L. West, *Ancient Greek Music*, 1992; scale reconstruction **[inferred]**]

Our synthesis (src/buildings/parthenon/music.js): Dorian species on E; a kithara (plucked) melody with occasional fifths; an aulos drone and slow aulos phrases in the open air; in the naos the kithara alone, slower, in a resonant room; the west room near silent.
