## 1. The real example

- **What:** the Great Church (Megale Ekklesia) of Holy Wisdom, cathedral of Constantinople. [stated: all sources]
- **Builders and dates:** begun in 532 after the earlier basilica burned in the Nika riots, dedicated 27 December 537 under Justinian I; architects Anthemius of Tralles and Isidore of Miletus. Earthquakes in 553 and 557; part of the dome fell in May 558; Isidore the Younger rebuilt it, higher and steeper, by 562 (rededicated 24 December 562). [stated: architecture-news; studiomatrx; madainproject; orthodoxwiki]
- **State shown:** the church of 562, with Justinian's interior: marble revetment and plain gold mosaic on the vaults, no figures; the chancel, ambo and silver furnishings of the sixth century. No Ottoman buttresses, minarets or furnishings. [stated: pallasweb, "the nave vaults were covered in plain gold mosaic, with no figures"; the later buttresses are Ottoman, madainproject]

## 2. Type and typology

A **domed basilica**: a central square covered by a dome on pendentives over four great arches and four main piers; east and west the arches open into semidomes, each stepping down over smaller semidomed exedrae, so that the nave reads as one long oval space under domes; aisles and galleries on three sides (north, south, west), separated from the nave by colonnades; an inner and an outer narthex across the west front, and an atrium before them. [stated: Britannica; fhw.gr — "two semi-domes and four exedrae buttress the dome on the east–west axis"; studiomatrx; hagiasophia.com]

## 3. Orientation and site

- Apse to the east, entrance from the west through the atrium and the two narthexes. (The real axis is turned about 33° south of east; here the axis is due east–west.) [stated: hagiasophia.com — the apse at the east end, the Imperial Door on the west; the deviation from true east inferred from the plan's bearing]
- On the first hill of the peninsula above the sea, on the north side of the Augustaion square, near the Great Palace. In our world: on a low promontory above the river (standing in for the Bosphorus) in the Byzantine region, north of Greece (docs/world.md). [stated: setting; position in our world inferred]

**Derived [inferred]** (local coordinates: origin at the centre of the dome on the nave floor, +x east, +z south):
- *Central square* 31.0 m between the faces of the four main piers (the dome's diameter). The great arches spring at **24.5 m** and rise 15.5 m to **40.0 m**, where the pendentives end and the dome begins. (The 120 ft of pallasweb would put the arch crowns at 36.6 m; with the crown of the dome at 55.6 m and a rise of the 562 dome of about 15 m, 40 m fits better.)
- *Pendentives* are the parts of one sphere, radius 31.0 / √2 = 21.92 m, centred on the nave axis at the springing (24.5 m), that lie inside the square and below 40.0 m; its sections in the four arch planes are exactly the four semicircular arches, and its section at 40.0 m is the dome's base circle (radius 15.5 m).
- *Dome:* a window zone 40.0–42.0 m with 40 windows (≈ 1.3 × 1.9 m) between 40 buttress piers; above it a cap of radius 15.63 m rising to 55.6 m, with 40 shallow ribs inside; cornice at 40.0 m.
- *Semidomes* east and west: quarter-spheres of radius 15.5 m centred on the east and west arch lines at 24.5 m, their rims the great arches; reaching x = ±31.0 m. Each is cut, at its base, by three vertical planes: the apse (east) or the west arch (west) on the axis (chord 10.4 m) and two exedrae on the diagonals (chord 12.0 m, centred 52° off the axis). The section of a sphere by a vertical plane is a circle, so each cut is a semicircular arch springing at 24.5 m.
- *Exedrae:* half-cylinders of radius 6.0 m under half-domes springing at 24.5 m; their curved walls are open in two arcades — two porphyry columns below (three arches, top 12.3 m), six verde antico columns above at the gallery (seven arches) — with a wall of five windows above (17.6–24.5 m).
- *Main piers:* 7.5 m (E–W) × 18.5 m (N–S), from the arch lines outward to the aisle walls (|x| 15.5–23.0, |z| 15.5–34.0), pierced at ground and gallery level by the aisles' passages.
- *North and south nave arcades:* under each great arch, four verde antico columns (10.4 m) with five arches below the gallery floor (14.6 m); six gallery columns (4.7 m) with seven arches above; the tympanum wall above (from 21.5 m to the arch) pierced by two rows of windows — seven below and five above (12 per tympanum, 24 in all).
- *Aisles* 16.5 m wide (|z| 16–34.5) on two storeys (ground floor 0–14.0, gallery 14.6–24.0), outer walls 1.5 m thick with two rows of windows; *west gallery* over the inner narthex.
- *Apse:* the bema (x 27–33.5, 10.4 m wide) and a semicircle of radius 5.2 m beyond, under its own semidome; the synthronon (stepped clergy bench) round the curve; three windows in each of two rows (six).
- *Inner narthex:* x −42.0…−32.5 (9.5 m), the full width (z ±30), nine bays; nine doors into the nave, the Imperial Door (≈ 4 × 7 m) in the middle. *Outer narthex:* x −47.5…−43.0, five doors. *Atrium:* x −95…−48, z ±24, colonnaded on four sides, a phiale (fountain) in the middle.
- *Ramp tower:* at the north-west corner beside the narthex, four flights round a solid core from the narthex to the west gallery (the real ramps are a continuous slope without steps; built here as stairs of 19 cm risers to keep the tower compact).
- *Chancel:* a screen of twelve columns across the bema mouth and along its sides; the altar under a ciborium on four columns; the *ambo* in the nave east of the dome's centre, an oval platform on eight posts within eight columns, joined to the chancel by the solea (a walled path).

## 5. Section and structure

Walls and piers of stone (piers) and thin brick in thick beds of mortar (the joints 1.5 times the brick); vaults and domes of light brick and lime mortar; the dome carried on pendentives over four great arches and four piers; thrust taken east and west by the semidomes and exedrae, north and south by the great buttresses across the aisles. The north and south walls lean; the piers deformed during the building, so more columns and arches were added in the galleries. [stated: studiomatrx; madainproject; pallasweb]

## 6. Sequence of spaces and use

1. **Atrium:** a colonnaded court with a fountain, open to the sky. [stated: madainproject; form inferred]
2. **Outer narthex** (exonarthex) → **inner narthex** (esonarthex): long vaulted halls across the front, gold above; the emperor entered the nave through the Imperial Door. [stated]
3. **Nave:** the dome — "suspended from heaven by a golden chain" (Procopius) — on its ring of windows; the semidomes and exedrae on the axis; the ambo in the middle where the readers and singers stood, the solea leading to the chancel and altar. [stated: studiomatrx (Procopius); fhw.gr; pallasweb]
4. **Aisles and galleries:** the south gallery for the emperor's household and clergy, the north for women, the west with the place of the empress; reached by ramps. [stated: pallasweb; hagiasophia.com]

## 7. Materials and finishes

- Marbles from across the empire: verde antico (green Thessalian) columns, red porphyry from Egypt (Gebel Dokhan), Proconnesian white marble with grey veins for the floor, banded alabaster; walls sheathed to the springing with book-matched marble revetment. [stated: pallasweb; Britannica; orthodoxwiki]
- Vaults, domes, semidomes and soffits: plain gold mosaic, with crosses; silver tesserae among the gold in dim places; tesserae set at angles to catch the light. [stated: pallasweb; Britannica; architecture-news]
- Exterior: plain stuccoed walls; the domes covered in lead [stated: orthodoxwiki for the stucco; lead roofing inferred: standard Byzantine practice].
- Furnishings: silver revetment of the chancel screen and ciborium. [stated: pallasweb — "giant silver-and-gold ciborium"]

## 8. Decorative programme (L3)

Justinian's church had no figurative mosaic: crosses and ornament on gold. Later figures (the apse Virgin, the Deesis, the emperors) are not of this state. L3: revetment patterns, the mosaic ornament of the soffits, the opus sectile of the floor, the furniture in silver. [stated: pallasweb; Britannica]

## 9. Lighting

- The ring of 40 windows at the base of the dome (the dome seems to float on light), windows in the tympana, the exedrae, the semidomes' bases, the apse and the outer walls of the aisles and galleries; translucent stone in some panes. [stated: Britannica; pallasweb]
- At night: thousands of glass oil lamps in hundreds of silver polykandela hung from the dome and arches, and a great ring of lamps under the dome. [stated: pallasweb; fhw.gr]
- Here: the region's morning sun (south-east) through the south windows and the dome's ring; lamps in rings under the dome and in the aisles (dim).

## 10. Surroundings

On a hill above the water; to the south the Augustaion (a square with porticoes), the Great Palace beyond. Gardens of cypress, plane trees, figs, vines and Judas trees; stone pines on the slopes. [stated: setting; planting inferred from the region's flora] **Built (L2):** the atrium; a paved square south of the church standing for the Augustaion; houses with tiled roofs round the hill (`src/world/features/`). *L4: the Great Palace, the Hippodrome, the Mese.*

**In the city (2026-10-28):** the first hill is scaled to the Byzantine district (14 m, its gentle side west toward the east avenue, from which the way climbs to the atrium); the district's blocks of tiled houses with jettied upper storeys stand round the hill.

## 11. Music

Byzantine chant: monophonic hymns and psalms, alternating soloists and choir, low and high voices, over an *ison* (a sustained drone on the mode's tonic); the eight modes (oktoechos); no instruments in the liturgy. The building's reverberation is very long — more than five times a modern concert hall; at the end of Psalm 140 the sound lingers for nearly 12 seconds; the dome responds most to the high frequencies; singers stretch out words to stay intelligible. [stated: Early Music America on Icons of Sound / Cappella Romana; the oktoechos: general musicology]

Our synthesis (`src/buildings/hagia-sophia/music.js`): voices (formant pads) singing a slow melody in the first mode (Dorian-like on D) over an ison on D; in the nave a 12-second reverberation; the narthex and atrium quieter, a semantron (wooden board, struck) occasionally outside.
