## 1. The real example

- **What:** the Amida Hall (Amida-dō) of Byōdō-in in Uji, south of Kyoto, called the Phoenix Hall (Hōō-dō) from the early Edo period; the best surviving hall of the Pure Land (Jōdo) type, made to be the palace of Amida's paradise on earth. [stated: Wikipedia; Bunka-isan Online]
- **Date:** dedicated in 1053 by the regent Fujiwara no Yorimichi (992–1074), a year after he made his father's villa at Uji a temple (1052), the first year of the "latter days of the Law" (mappō). [stated: Bunka-isan Online; tabi-mag]
- **State shown:** as dedicated in 1053, painted red, with its original statue, canopy and bodhisattvas; the garden as restored (the pond dredged and its pebble shores relaid after the excavations of the 1990s). [stated: dates — Wikipedia; colour and shores inferred, below]

## 2. Type and typology

A Buddhist hall of the Amida type built like the palace of a noble's mansion (*shinden*): a central hall (*chūdō*) with flanking wing corridors (*yokurō*) and a tail corridor (*birō*) behind, symmetrical, "a phoenix about to take flight". [stated: JAANUS; Bunka-isan Online] The four parts are National Treasures (1897). [stated: Bunka-isan Online]

## 3. Orientation and site

- The hall faces **east**, across the pond Aji-ike, so that it is seen from the east bank as the Pure Land in the west, and catches the morning sun over the hills beyond the Uji River. [stated: Wikipedia; MLIT 001560861]
- It stands on an island in the pond; its image is doubled in the water, so that the hall seems to float. [stated: reflection — MLIT 001560861; island inferred from the garden's plan]
- In our world: in a new region, Heian Japan, north of Tang China over the pass of Mount Wutai: a green river plain under wooded hills, the hall on its island on the west bank of the river, its front to the river and the sunrise (docs/world.md). [inferred]

## 5. Section and structure

A timber frame: the core's round columns carry three-stepped bracket sets and the beams of the main roof, its rafters hidden above the coffered ceiling; the pent roof is carried by square posts on its own small brackets; the wings are frames of posts and beams with a floor at mid-height. [stated: JAANUS] The whole on stone podiums. [inferred]

## 6. Sequence of spaces and use

1. **The view across the pond** from the east bank: the hall, its wings and its reflection; through the raised middle of the pent roof and the lattice window, Amida's face. [stated: JAANUS; MLIT]
2. **The bridge** to the island and the walk round the hall on its podium, under the wings. [inferred]
3. **The central hall:** the statue under its canopy, the bodhisattvas on clouds on the walls above, the painted doors. [stated]

## 7. Materials and finishes

Timber painted red (*ni-nuri*, red earth pigment) with white walls and yellow ochre on the rafter ends [inferred: Heian practice, as restored in 2012–14]; grey clay tiles; gilt bronze phoenixes and fittings; the statue gilt over lacquer [stated: MLIT 001553420]; inside, painted patterns, mother-of-pearl inlay on the canopy and altar [stated: Bunka-isan Online, "絵画、文様、螺鈿"].

## 8. Decorative programme (L3)

The door and wall paintings of Amida's descent (*raigō*), an early example of Yamato-e with the landscape round Kyoto [stated: Wikipedia]; the 52 bodhisattvas playing music and dancing on clouds; the openwork nimbus with flying figures; the canopy's inlay; the painted flowers (*hōsōge*) of beams and ceiling.

## 9. Lighting

The hall faces the sunrise: the region's early-morning sun, low in the east-south-east across the river, lights its face and the statue through the open doors; mist on the water at times. [stated: orientation; time chosen, inferred]

## 10. Surroundings

The Uji River close by on the east, wooded hills on the west; the pond with its island and pebble shores; Japanese red pines, maples (in autumn colour), cryptomerias and bamboo groves on the hills; reeds by the river; rice fields on the plain; a village of wooden houses with tiled and bark roofs. **In the city (2026-10-28):** the pond and island stand in the Heian district of the compact city; timber houses under bark and thatch fill its blocks, with red pines, maples and bamboo in the gardens; the Uji River and the hills are not shown.

## 11. Music

The music of the Heian court, *gagaku* (*tōgaku*): the mouth organ *shō* holding clusters, the double-reed *hichiriki* and the flute *ryūteki* in a slow heterophonic melody, the *biwa* and *koto* marking the beats; Buddhist chant (*shōmyō*) in the halls. [general musicology, e.g. W. P. Malm, *Traditional Japanese Music and Musical Instruments*] Our synthesis (`src/buildings/byodo-in-phoenix-hall/music.js`): outside, sustained shō-like clusters, a flute melody and a plucked koto on a pentatonic mode; inside, a chanting voice and a small bell.
