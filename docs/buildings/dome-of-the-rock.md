## 1. The real example

- **What:** Qubbat al-Sakhra, the shrine over the Rock (al-Sakhra) on the Haram al-Sharif (Temple Mount), Jerusalem — the oldest standing major monument of Islamic architecture. [stated: Wikipedia; Studio Matrx]
- **Builders and dates:** ordered by the Umayyad caliph Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan; begun 685/86 or 688, the dedicatory inscription dated AH 72 (691/92); the works overseen by Raja ibn Haywah and Yazid ibn Salam; architect unknown. [stated: Madain Project; Studio Matrx; Wikipedia]
- **State shown:** as completed in 691/92: marble revetment on the lower outer walls and glass mosaic on the upper, the gilded dome, the Umayyad interior. Not shown: the Ottoman tiles (1545–52), the rebuilt dome of 1022–23, the later windows and the qanatir (arched gates) at the stairs of the platform. [stated: Wikipedia; Studio Matrx; vitrosearch]

## 2. Type and typology

A centralised shrine: an octagon of outer walls, inside it an octagonal arcade of 8 piers and 16 columns, inside that a circular arcade of 4 piers and 12 columns carrying a high drum and a timber dome over the Rock; two concentric ambulatories for circumambulation. The geometry is set out from a circle and two rotated squares. Its model is the late Roman and Byzantine domed martyrium (the Anastasis rotunda of the Holy Sepulchre has a dome of almost the same size: 20.90 m against 20.20 m). [stated: Studio Matrx; Wikipedia, citing Creswell]

## 3. Orientation and site

- Four doors, one on each cardinal side; the octagon's sides face the cardinal points. [stated: Wikipedia]
- On a raised platform (about 4 m above the rest of the esplanade, reached by eight flights of steps) within the great walled esplanade of the Haram on Mount Moriah; the platform is not centred on the Haram. East of it, the Dome of the Chain (also of 691–92). [stated: Madain Project; Wikipedia]
- In our world: in a new region, the Levant, north of Byzantium over a pass; the esplanade on a flat-topped hill above the Kidron valley, the Mount of Olives across it (docs/world.md). [inferred]

## 5. Section and structure

Masonry walls and piers (stone), reused marble columns on tall bases tied by timber beams (sheathed in gilded metal), round arches with alternating light and dark voussoirs (ablaq); a drum carrying a double-shell timber dome braced by radiating ribs between the shells; sloping roofs over the ambulatories. [stated: Studio Matrx; Wikipedia]

## 6. Sequence of spaces and use

1. **The esplanade and the platform:** stairs up from the Haram to the platform; the Dome of the Chain to the east. [stated]
2. **Porch and door** on any of the four sides. [stated: doors; porches inferred]
3. **Outer ambulatory** between the outer walls and the octagonal arcade; **inner ambulatory** between the octagonal and circular arcades — walked round the Rock (circumambulation). [stated: Studio Matrx]
4. **The Rock** under the dome, seen through the circular arcade; a screen between the columns [inferred: a low barrier; the later Crusader grille is not shown].

## 7. Materials and finishes

- Outside: marble panels on the lower walls, glass mosaic on the upper walls (only fragments survive); a gilded dome. [stated: Wikipedia] Shown with a green glass ground [inferred: the fragments' colours are not recorded in our sources; L3].
- Inside: marble revetment on piers and walls; glass mosaic (≈ 1,200 m²) on gold ground — vegetal scrolls, trees, jewelled vessels, crowns, no living figures — on the arcades' soffits and spandrels, the drum; the inscription band of gold Qur'anic text; reused coloured marble columns with carved capitals; gilded metal plaques over the tie-beams. [stated: Studio Matrx; Wikipedia]
- Ceilings: painted and gilded timber [inferred: Umayyad practice; the present ones are later].

## 8. Decorative programme (L3)

The mosaic ornament (acanthus scrolls, vases, jewels, trees), the inscription band, the ablaq voussoirs, the marble panel patterns, window grilles.

## 9. Lighting

36 windows in the outer walls and 16 in the drum; light falls in the outer ambulatory and through the drum onto the Rock and the gold of the drum and dome; the inner ambulatory is darker. [stated: windows; distribution inferred] Here: the region's morning sun from the east-south-east.

## 10. Surroundings

The esplanade of the Haram on Mount Moriah, with olive trees and cypresses; the Kidron valley and the Mount of Olives to the east; the city on its hills west and south: houses of stone with flat roofs and small domes [inferred: the vernacular of the Levant, vaulted rooms with domed roofs]. **Built (L2):** the platform with its stairs, the Dome of the Chain, the esplanade, the town (`src/world/features/`). *L4: the Aqsa mosque, the Haram's walls and gates.*

## 11. Music

In the shrine no instruments: the human voice — the call to prayer and the recitation of the Qur'an, unaccompanied, melismatic. In the Umayyad town: the oud (lute), the reed flute (ney/qasaba) and the frame drum (daff). [general musicology; e.g. H. G. Farmer, *A History of Arabian Music* (1929)] Our synthesis (`src/buildings/dome-of-the-rock/music.js`): inside, a single voice in a slow, ornamented line over a soft drone, in a mode with a neutral (three-quarter-tone) second (Bayati-like, on D); outside, the oud and the reed flute with the frame drum. The modern maqam system is later; the neutral intervals are attested in early Arab theory (Farmer). [inferred]
